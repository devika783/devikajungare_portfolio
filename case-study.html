Voice-Assisted Companion for Alzheimer's Care

Designing an emotionally intelligent VUI to support patients and caregivers.

Roles

VUI Designer, UX Researcher, Conversation Designer

Phases

Research, Conversation Flow, IA, Prototyping, Testing

Target Users

Alzheimer's Patients & Caregivers

Platform

Voice-First Interface (VUI)

Duration

3 Weeks

Understanding the Problem

1. Problem Statement

Alzheimer's patients struggle with traditional digital systems due to cognitive decline, while caregivers face continuous safety monitoring. Existing voice assistants lack emotional and cognitive-safe interactions, creating an opportunity for a voice-first support system.

2. Design Challenge

How might we design a voice-first system that helps Alzheimer's patients stay calm, oriented, and safe while reducing caregiver monitoring stress?

3. Why Voice?

Voice interaction reduces cognitive effort because it:

  • requires no navigation
  • feels natural and familiar
  • supports real-time reassurance
  • works during confusion or panic

Research Insights

Evening hours increase agitation.

Simple instructions reduce confusion.

Patients struggle to recall routines and important events.

Patients often repeat questions due to anxiety.

Emotional reassurance works better than correction.

Caregivers need alerts, not monitoring systems.

User Personas

Understanding the dual-user ecosystem: The Patient and The Caregiver.

Early-Mid Stage Scenario

PATIENT-A: Rajesh (68)

"Sometimes I forget where I am, and it makes me anxious."

  • Needs: Emotional reassurance, familiar routines, gentle reminders
  • Pain Points: Forgetfulness, Anxiety, Disorientation

Design Implication: Voice must be calm, repetitive, and never corrective.

CAREGIVER-A: (Age 42)

"I worry constantly about whether they are safe."

  • Needs: Safety alerts, Emotional peace, Remote monitoring
  • Pain Points: Constant worry, Burnout, Lack of control

Design Implication: System should notify only when necessary.

Mid-Stage Scenario

PATIENT-B: Nitin (74)

"Sometimes I feel lost, and it scares me."

  • Needs: Strong emotional grounding, step-by-step task guidance, wandering prevention, night-time reassurance
  • Pain Points: Frequent confusion, Evening agitation (sundowning), False beliefs or paranoia, Attempting to leave home

Design Implication: Voice must activate safety mode during evening hours, increase grounding statements, avoid contradiction, and trigger caregiver escalation when risk thresholds are crossed.

CAREGIVER-B: (Age 46)

"I'm always worried something might happen when I'm not around."

  • Needs: Real-time safety alerts, Emotional reassurance for father, Routine automation, Reduced monitoring burden
  • Pain Points: Fear of wandering incidents, Sleep disruption, Emotional burnout, Balancing job and caregiving

Design Implication: Send alerts only during high-risk situations to reduce caregiver fatigue.

Patient Experience Journey

How Saathi supports patients through moments of confusion, repetition, and disorientation.

Confusion
Patient asks where they are.
"You are at home. You are safe."
Repetition
Patient repeats the same question.
Responds calmly with the same answer.
Task Confusion
Patient forgets what they were doing.
Guides step-by-step.
Wandering
Patient attempts to leave home.
Provides verbal redirection and alerts caregiver.
Night Disorientation
Patient wakes at night confused.
Activates whisper mode and reassures.

Conversational Design Principles

Designing a voice that heals, guides, and reassures.

Give one instruction at a time. Use short, simple sentences. Repeat calmly without frustration. Validate emotions before correcting. Avoid directly contradicting the user.

Example Voice Interactions

How Saathi responds to common situations experienced by Alzheimer's patients.

Patient: "Where am I?"
Saathi: "You are at home. You are safe."
Patient: "Where is Neha?"
Saathi: "Neha is at work. She will return later."
Patient: "What was I doing?"
Saathi: "You were washing your hands. Let's continue."
Patient: "I feel confused."
Saathi: "I am here with you. Everything is okay."

Safety Support & System Limitations

Safety Support

The system detects risk situations like wandering and sends instant alerts to caregivers, allowing quick response without requiring dashboards or continuous monitoring.

System Limitations

Not a medical diagnostic tool

Designed for early-to-mid stage Alzheimer's

Some alerts may be triggered to prioritize safety

Project Reflection
